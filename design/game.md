# Pixel TD: game overview

Status: **draft for the owner's review** (issue #3). Decided in the question rounds of 2026-11-18.
Pixel TD is a codename; the real name comes later.

Big systems have their own docs:
- [world.md](world.md): world layout and the map generator.
- [towers.md](towers.md): the tower system.
- `test-arena.md`: the test arena and live tuning (not written yet).

## Purpose

An endless roguelite tower defense game. You hold a planet-surface outpost against escalating
waves until you fall. Every run is a fresh generated map, and you build your defense from parts
drafted during the run. It should feel different each run, and stay easy to hold in mind.

## Rules

**Run structure**
- Endless: a run lasts until you fall. There's no win screen.
- One map per run, generated from a random seed. Normal runs never show or accept a seed; the
  test arena does.
- Between waves you choose from three kinds of thing:
  - a **tower draft**, which unlocks parts ([towers.md](towers.md));
  - **perks and modifiers**;
  - **risk/reward events**.
- **No meta-progression:** nothing carries over between runs.

**Setting:** retro-futuristic sci-fi. The defense is a planet-surface outpost: a core (a reactor or
beacon) near the centre of the map holds against enemies coming from seeded spawns, at the map's
edges or inside it.

**View**
- Flat top-down, looking straight down.
- Pixel-perfect: the world is drawn 1:1 and shown at a whole-number scale. The view fills the
  window, and the default zoom shows about 640x360 world pixels. Zooming out past 1x, to 1/2 and
  then the whole map, is the one exception: those steps are drawn shrunk and smooth.
- The map is 5120 x 2880 px: four 1440p screens in a 2 x 2 layout, or 64 views at the default zoom.
  The camera pans and zooms in whole-number steps, then out to the whole map ([world.md](world.md)).
- The terrain is a seamless heightmap: brightness shows height, and hue shows the ground material.
- A hidden grid of 16 px cells (320 x 180) sits under it, for terrain and pathfinding.
- Towers and the core snap to an 8 px placement subgrid.
  - A light tower is 3x3 subcells (24 px).
  - A heavy tower is 6x6 (48 px).
  - The core is 9x9 (72 px).

**Art**
- Every sprite is generated by Claude: text pixel sources become a PNG atlas through a Node tool.
  Nothing is hand-drawn or downloaded.
- The exact retro-futuristic style is still open. A style board will show 5–6 directions using the
  same sprites.

**Audio:** silent in v0.1.

## How towers get designed

Bastion Line's towers were too many to hold in mind and slow to design. Pixel TD changes both the
tower model ([towers.md](towers.md)) and the process.

1. **Pitch and pick.**
   - The owner describes a role or a feeling.
   - Claude pitches 3 variants on a **short pitch page**: sprites, a one-line role each and a few
     numbers.
   - The owner picks one.
2. **Live tuning.**
   - Part numbers live in a data file that the game hot-reloads.
   - A developer overlay shows sliders.
   - Tuning happens by feel, during a wave.
3. **Test arena.**
   - A sandbox on a real generated map: place any tower for free, spawn any enemy or wave,
     pause and step.
   - It shows range and damage-per-second readouts.

## v0.1 build order

The owner chose **arena first**: towers are designed in the game from the start. The arena uses the
real map generator, so the generator comes first.

1. **Map generator** ([world.md](world.md)):
   - the heightmap: hills, mesas, cliffs and rock formations;
   - the seeded core near the centre and 3 seeded spawns;
   - line of sight and height-based range;
   - fog;
   - the camera.
2. **Test arena + live tuning** (`test-arena.md`), together with **enemy pathfinding and mazing**
   (issue #11): enemies find their own way, and towers shape the route. Both still to be designed
   in their own question rounds.
3. **First parts**, designed by pitch and pick and tuned in the arena ([towers.md](towers.md)).
4. **Minimal run loop:** waves, the draft, gold and the core falling.

## Scope

- **v0.1, First playable:** the four steps above.
- **v0.2, Roguelite loop:**
  - perks, modifiers and events;
  - richer spawns and maps in later versions (the owner's rule: start simple).
- **v0.3, Art & polish:** the chosen art style throughout.
